# How far a Medicare Advantage network can send you

CMS's maximum drive times for Medicare Advantage networks (HSD Reference File for plan year 2026), by county and specialty, compared with the estimated drive from residents 65+ to the nearest provider of that specialty who treats Medicare patients.

**Live:** https://usnish.xyz/ma-network-standards/

## Sources

- The rule: [42 CFR 422.116](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-C/section-422.116) (eCFR text as of November 2026; last amended June 2025). (d)(4): at least 90% of the Medicare beneficiaries residing in Large Metro and Metro counties, and at least 85% in Micro, Rural and CEAC counties, must live within the time and distance limits of an in-network provider. (d)(5): a 10-percentage-point credit when the plan also contracts telehealth providers, for a list that includes primary care, cardiology, psychiatry, neurology, nephrology and endocrinology (not oncology, pulmonology, hospitals or nursing facilities). CMS measures this against its MA Medicare Sample Census ((d)(1)(i), (d)(3)(i)), not the plan's enrollee list. (d)(3): where supply or utilization requires it, CMS raises (never lowers) a county's limit to the distance at which 90% of the population reaches a provider, rounded up to the next 5. (d)(6) and (d)(7) give further credits for certificate-of-need states and new applicants; these are not modeled.
- The limits: CMS 2026 HSD Reference File (`2026-hsd-reference-file-updated-12-17-2025.xlsx`; provider and facility time & distance sheets). Its "Criteria Changes" sheet compares 2025 with 2026, so it governs plan year 2026. The file itself does not state the 85/90% thresholds; they come from the regulation. Rebuild when the plan year 2027 file is posted (one URL in `_viz/malib.py`).
- CMS Doctors and Clinicians national downloadable file (practice ZIP codes; release of October 30, 2026, the resource stamp in the URL, recorded as `clinicians_release`), CMS Hospital General Information (acute care and critical access hospitals, Census-geocoded), CMS Nursing Home Provider Information (coordinates; Medicare-certified only, so the 245 Medicaid-only facilities are left out, leaving 14,441), Census 2020 tract population centers, CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022 (population 65+), USDA 2023 Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, OSRM driving times (calibration and the top-10 check).

## Method

- Nearest provider to each tract's population center, straight-line km converted to minutes with `_viz/drive_model.json`, capped at 240 minutes; county figures weighted by population 65+. HSD counties are keyed by SSA code and translated to FIPS through the CMS enrollment file.
- Clinicians are placed at the center of each practice ZIP code they are listed at. Counts shown are unique clinicians (NPI). Cardiology, for example: 29,764 cardiologists, 76,140 clinician-and-ZIP listings, 6,880 ZIP codes.
- Visiting clinics are left out of the distances: a ZIP code is dropped for a specialty when every clinician of that specialty listed there is also listed at a ZIP code more than 100 km away that has more clinicians of that specialty. For cardiology this drops 891 of 6,880 ZIP codes, and the rural average drive to the nearest cardiologist is 36 minutes (28 with those sites counted). The summary keeps both (`near_mean`, `near_mean_with_visiting`).
- "Share of beneficiaries allowed beyond the limit" = 100 − the (d)(4) threshold for the county type, + 10 for (d)(5) specialties when the plan offers telehealth. Rural cardiology: 100 − 85 + 10 = 25%, or 1 in 4. The CON and new-applicant credits ((d)(6)–(7)) can add more and are not modeled.
- The "past the limit" share is not published for primary care: its 10–40 minute limits are within the error of the drive model (fixed start of about 7–8 minutes) and ZIP-code placement.

Rebuild: `python3 _viz/calibrate_drive.py` (once), then `uv run --with numpy --with scipy --with openpyxl python3 ma-network-standards/scripts/build_data.py`. The build also writes `data/top10_check.csv` and caches its OSRM routes in `_viz/cache/ma-network/`.

## Top-10 check (cardiology, November 2026 build)

`data/top10_check.csv` lists the top 10 counties in each of the four rankings (Alaska left out, as on the page), the ZIP code the county's largest 65+ tract is routed to, how many cardiologists are listed there and how many of them are visitors by the rule above, and a real OSRM driving time for that trip.

- Extra drive allowed (the default view): before the visiting-clinic rule, 7 of the top 10 rested on a ZIP code where every listed cardiologist was based far away (White Pine NV, Daniels MT, Garfield UT, Socorro NM, Liberty MT, Sherman KS, Valley NE). White Pine, for example, was routed to two Las Vegas cardiologists listed at the hospital in Ely. After the rule none do, but 4 of the top 10 still rest on small-town sites where most listed cardiologists are flagged as visitors (Barnes ND 12 of 16, Box Butte NE 21 of 22, Ramsey ND 12 of 18, Stutsman ND 16 of 17). Each site survives because at least one of its cardiologists is not flagged: their city practice is under 100 km away, or lists fewer cardiologists than the town site. These are most likely part-time clinics. Cheyenne CO and Apache AZ are routed to large rural ZIP codes where the model is 32 and 41 minutes shorter than the real route. For the other trips in this ranking the model is within 10 minutes of OSRM.
- Drive the rules allow: these are CMS's own numbers and cannot be outreach artefacts.
- Drive to the nearest: the top 5 are at the 240-minute cap, and on these long trips the model is off by a median of 34 minutes either way (Jones SD 235 vs 139 real, on I-90; Garfield MT capped at 240 vs 307 real).
- Nearest past the limit: all top 10 are at 100%, so their order is arbitrary. The model is mostly shorter than the real routes here (Inyo CA 141 vs 379 minutes across the Sierra), so these counties are, if anything, farther past the limit.

## What changed (November 2026)

- The rule: the page said a network passes if 90% of members live within the limit. Corrected to 90% in Large Metro and Metro counties and 85% in Micro, Rural and CEAC counties, with the 10-point telehealth credit for listed specialties, citing 42 CFR 422.116(d)(4)–(5). The headline now leads with the rural example, computed from the rule: a plan with telehealth can leave 1 in 4 beneficiaries beyond the 75-minute rural cardiology limit and still pass.
- Provider counts: the "Providers 71,435" card counted clinician-and-ZIP pairs. It now shows unique clinicians and practice ZIP codes, and visiting-only sites are left out of the distances (see above). The 2026 clinician file was re-downloaded, so the counts differ from the old build.
- Primary care "past the limit" is no longer shipped or shown.
- Stat cards now match the rural headline (rural limit, members allowed beyond it, rural average nearest, clinician count) and label the plan year. A rule column and note were added to the rural table, and the rule is stated in the bar-chart note and tooltips.
- Removed the link to the retired clinical trial deserts page.
- Review fixes: the 85/90% rule now counts the Medicare beneficiaries living in the county (as (d)(4) says), not the plan's members, everywhere on the page. The clinician file URL points at CMS's current release (October 30, 2026; the old August URL now returns 404) and the page states that release date instead of the download month; the cached file was byte-identical to it, so no clinician counts changed. Medicaid-only nursing facilities are left out (14,686 to 14,441 nursing homes; rural average to the nearest moves from 17.4 to 17.6 minutes). The chart note's list of where "extreme access" counties are is now computed (mostly Great Plains; 20 of Alaska's 23). The customization rule is stated as in (d)(3). On the map, counties whose average nearest provider is already past the limit get their own color instead of the lightest blue.
