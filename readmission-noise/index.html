Noisy ruler, real differences

Medicare's Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program cuts payments to hospitals whose risk-adjusted 30-day readmissions, on any of six measures, are higher than the median hospital in their peer group. How much of any one hospital's penalty is measurement noise, and are the differences underneath real? Short answer: a single year's penalty is a noisy ruler, but for heart failure, heart attack and COPD the differences it measures look mostly stable.

Whether a hospital is penalized for a measure comes down to one comparison: is its excess readmission ratio above its peer group's median?

1Can you spot the real data?

Each panel plots hospitals' excess readmission ratio (ERR: CMS's predicted readmissions divided by expected, so 1.0 is average) against their number of eligible discharges. One panel is CMS's real fiscal 2026 data. The other eight are chance-only: every hospital is set at exactly its expected readmission rate, readmissions are drawn at random, and the result is shrunk the way this page's fit to CMS's model shrinks it. Pick the panel you think is real.

    4A design that penalizes most hospitals

    How this was built

    Data. CMS FY2026 HRRP supplemental file (final rule; discharges July 2021 to June 2024): each hospital's ERR, peer-group median, penalty flag, DRG payment ratio and payment reduction for six measures. CMS FY2023 supplemental file (July 2018 to November 2019 and July 2020 to June 2021; CMS excluded the first half of 2020), the most recent window that does not overlap. CMS Provider Data Catalog hospital-level FY2026 readmissions (dataset 9n3s-kdb3, via its datastore API): names and predicted and expected readmission rates. FY2027 penalties shown in the hospital card come from the FY2027 supplemental file. All are public downloads.

    Emulating CMS's shrinkage. CMS estimates each hospital's readmission rate with a hierarchical logistic model that pulls small hospitals toward the national average. This page emulates that with one number per measure, k: predicted − expected = n/(n+k) × (observed/n − expected), fitted to CMS's own published predicted and expected rates. A hospital's reliability is n/(n+k); a hospital with k discharges keeps half of its raw result. The fit and the 95% bootstrap range of k (400 resamples of hospitals):

    Readmission counts under 11 are suppressed, so k is fitted on larger hospitals. As a check on smaller ones, inverting the fitted shrinkage for the hospitals with suppressed counts recovers an implied count between 0 and 10 for of them.

    Lineup. In each chance-only panel, every hospital with 25 or more discharges and a published expected rate gets readmissions drawn from Binomial(n, expected rate), shrunk with the fitted k, and turned back into an ERR. The real panel's position is shuffled on every load. Because published ERRs are already shrunk, chance spread is narrow for small hospitals (heavily shrunk), widest near k discharges, and slowly narrower for large ones (less noise): a lens, not a funnel. The shaded band drawn after you pick is the middle 95% of chance-only ERRs at each size for a hospital with the typical expected rate.

    Probability above the peer median. Normal approximation to the posterior for the hospital's true ratio: mean = the published ERR, variance = τ² × (1 − reliability), where τ² = (1 − expected rate) / (expected rate × k) is the spread of true hospital ratios that the same shrinkage implies. The 90% range in the card is the published ERR ± 1.645 posterior SDs. To check calibration, the build simulates hospitals that follow these assumptions, recomputes ERRs, peer medians and probabilities, and compares stated probabilities with how often the true ratio really was above the median: Using either end of k's bootstrap range, the share of penalized heart failure hospitals with a 90% or higher probability stays .

    Noise-only ceiling. For hospitals with 25 or more discharges in both windows, the build draws a true ratio for each hospital (variance τ²), keeps it fixed in both windows, simulates readmissions with each window's real discharge count, applies the same shrinkage, and records the correlation and the share penalized again (300 runs). FY2023 expected rates are not in the files, so each hospital's FY2026 expected rate is used for both windows, and FY2026's k is applied to FY2023. That assumption matches the spread of FY2023's published ERRs for heart failure, heart attack, COPD and bypass surgery (within 10%), but not for hip/knee replacement, where the same k predicts a wider spread than CMS published (). As a sensitivity check, the build also fits a separate k for FY2023 that matches its published spread and reruns the ceiling with it: The observed correlation divided by the ceiling estimates how strongly the true ratios themselves agree across the two windows.

    Caveats.

    • This page emulates CMS's hierarchical logistic model with n/(n+k); the fit to CMS's published predicted and expected rates is close (R² above 0.99) but it is not CMS's model. The bootstrap range of k reflects only sampling uncertainty in that fit.
    • The chance-only panels apply today's shrinkage to pure noise. In a world where hospitals truly were identical, CMS's model would estimate less true variation and shrink further, so read them as the noise floor of published ERRs, not as what CMS would publish.
    • Readmission counts under 11 are suppressed, so k is fitted on larger hospitals, which are the least noisy.
    • Both windows are claims data that include COVID-era exclusions and shifts in case mix (FY2023: mid-2018 to mid-2021 with Dec 2019–Jun 2020 excluded; FY2026: mid-2021 to mid-2024). From FY2027, CMS adds Medicare Advantage patients to these measures and uses a two-year window.
    • ERR is a hospital-level ratio, not any patient's risk. Peer groups are quintiles of each hospital's share of patients dually eligible for Medicare and Medicaid, and the penalty threshold is that group's median, so the same ERR can be penalized in one group and not another.
    • Probabilities assume CMS's risk adjustment is right. Differences it misses (social risk beyond dual eligibility, local post-acute care) count here as "true" differences between hospitals, not as noise.

    Prior work. Others have shown the noise in these measures: Thompson MP et al., Health Serv Res 2016 (PMC5134200), on the reliability of HRRP measures; Shen C, Wadhera RK, Yeh RW, JAMA Cardiol 2021, on penalty misclassification; Wilcock AD et al., PLOS ONE 2021, "Luck of the draw"; and Joshi S et al., JAMA Intern Med 2019, on regression to the mean. Those studies are static and use older data. This page adds current (FY2026) data, a per-hospital view, a lineup test, and a check of year-to-year persistence against the ceiling that noise allows.
