A rule in motion

    What people are still owed

    This rule is about written translation of plan documents. Below the line, enrollees are not left with nothing. Every plan must have free interpreters on its customer-service lines (42 CFR 422.111(h)(1)(iii); 423.128(d)(1)(iii)). Once a plan knows a member's language, it must keep sending materials in it, but only if that is one of the plan's threshold languages (422.2267(a)(3); 423.2267(a)(3)). Below the line, any written translation depends on Section 1557's general duty to give meaningful access (45 CFR 92.201) and on the Office for Civil Rights' notice that free language help exists, in at least the 15 languages most common among people with limited English in the state (45 CFR 92.11). Some carriers translate more than they must, California's managed-care rules set their own threshold languages, and plans that combine Medicare and Medicaid follow the state's Medicaid translation standard as well (422.2267(a)(4)). This page counts only what the federal 5% rule requires.

    What could change this

    About the data

    The rule. Medicare Advantage and Part D plans "must translate required materials into any non-English language that is the primary language of at least 5 percent of the individuals in a plan benefit package (PBP) service area" (42 CFR 422.2267(a)(2); 423.2267(a)(2) for drug plans). Required materials include the Evidence of Coverage, the Annual Notice of Change, the Notice of Denial of Medical Coverage or Payment, appeal notices and the formulary (422.2267(e)).

    Languages. American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, table C16001 (language spoken at home by ability to speak English, people 5 and older) for counties and the states, downloaded from the Census Bureau's table-based summary file. Puerto Rico is left out (CMS requires Spanish there). Table B16001 (detailed languages, states and public use microdata areas) feeds "Your language"; table B16004 (by age) feeds the age panel. The six languages on the guessing chart are the only single languages C16001 reports by county; it lumps the rest (French with Haitian, Russian with Polish, and so on).

    Two readings of "primary language". CMS's published method (HPMS memo of July 15, 2011, Appendix A) divides the people who speak a language and speak English less than "very well" by all people 5 and older in the plan's counties or states. CMS's current step-by-step notes are only in its plan portal (HPMS), and its 2025 memo describes "the percentage of non-English speakers", so this page also shows the most generous reading: everyone who speaks the language at home, however well they speak English. CMS's own count favors the first reading.

    Service areas. "Real 2027 plans" uses the counties every 2027 individual plan serves, from the CMS plan benefit package files (PlanArea for local plans; PlanRegionArea for regional PPOs and stand-alone drug plans). A plan's share pools all its counties, as CMS's method does; partial counties count whole. A person counts as reached when at least one plan that anyone with Medicare can join in their county (not special needs plans, PACE or employer plans) must translate into their language. "Plans people join" instead weights each county by the share of its September 2026 individual Medicare Advantage and drug plan members (CMS enrollment by contract, plan and county; counts under 11 are suppressed) who sit in a plan that must translate, using 2026 service areas; it shows what happens if people with limited English join plans the way their neighbors do. "Each county alone" treats every county of 10,000 or more people as its own plan; it is not an upper bound, since a plan that pools two heavily Chinese-speaking counties can carry a third along. "Each state alone" treats every state as one plan, as a stand-alone drug plan region mostly is. Connecticut's census data, now reported by planning region, were rebuilt into its eight old counties from census tracts to match CMS's county codes.

    Margins of error. County shares carry the Census Bureau's 90% margins of error, computed with its formula for a proportion. The guessing chart's county view counts a county only when its estimate is at or above 5%;

    Caveats. This page replicates CMS's published method; CMS's own list of each plan's required languages sits in HPMS, is not public and may use an older census release. That could move plans near the line, but not the zero for Vietnamese, Korean, Tagalog and Arabic: "Speaks at home" stands in for "primary language". The census counts everyone 5 and older, not people with Medicare, who are older and may differ in where they live and how well they speak English. Plan counts include plans that are pending. Every figure is as of the 2027 plan year and the rules in force on November 12, 2026; CMS has asked about changing the rule, so check for a 2028 proposed rule. Justice in Aging (January 2026 comment) and CMS itself (88 FR 22227, which counts contracts) have made the qualitative point that few languages meet the line; this page measures it in people, language by language.
