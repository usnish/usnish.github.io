One high reading

If a patient has one high blood pressure reading and nothing is done, how much lower will the next visit be? A national survey measured U.S. adults on two occasions about a month apart, with no treatment change recorded in between. Guess first, then see.

Where the drop comes from

The fall from one high home reading to the exam-centre average, split three ways. Setting offset: everyone read a little higher at home than at the exam centre, high or not, so that part is a measurement difference. Same visit: how much of the first reading's excess had already faded by readings 2 and 3 that same day. Second day: what was left to fade only by the next occasion. The last two are mostly regression to the mean.

How this was built

Data. NHANES III (), household adult file (adult.dat) and examination file (exam.dat), public-use files from the CDC National Center for Health Statistics. At the household interview, a trained interviewer took three systolic readings a minute apart after five minutes' seated rest, with a mercury-gravity sphygmomanometer (household interviewer BP manual). At the mobile exam centre, a physician took three more (MMWR 1995). The modern panel uses NHANES 2021–23 oscillometric readings (BPXO_L) with demographics (DEMO_L).

Caveats.

  • Different observer and setting. The first reading was taken at home by an interviewer and the second set at the exam centre by a physician, both with mercury devices, . The change adds variance, so the regression shown may overstate what a same-clinic recheck by the same nurse would show.
  • The offset correction assumes the home-vs-centre gap is the same at every level. By the average of the two measurements it ranges from to mmHg (). That is why raw and corrected falls are both shown, and why the reverse check matters: a level-dependent home/clinic difference would not also produce a fall when people are picked at the clinic.
  • "No BP diagnosis reported" is self-report (doctor ever said high blood pressure: no), not "untreated". At the end of the home visit the interviewer read each person a short statement about their result so they could seek care if it was high, and treatment started between visits was not recorded. Medication timing on the day of each measurement is also unknown. The reverse check, which runs the other way, is hard to explain by new treatment.
  • Share now under 140. Comparing one reading with a three-reading average exaggerates reclassification, so it is not used as a headline here. Adjusted for the offset,
  • Era and devices. The pattern is statistical and applies to any noisy measurement, but the exact numbers belong to this protocol (mercury readings to the nearest even number, 1988–94). The 2021–23 panel uses automated oscillometric cuffs at a single visit.
  • Point estimates are weighted; confidence intervals are shown only for the headline numbers. The longer-gap subgroup is small ().
  • No causal claims beyond this: no treatment change was recorded between the two visits.

Guideline. The 2017 ACC/AHA blood pressure guideline (since updated in 2025, Hypertension 2025) recommends proper methods for accurate measurement and documentation of BP … including averaging readings taken on ≥2 occasions (Class I), and asks clinicians to base estimates on an average of at least 2 readings obtained on at least 2 separate occasions, as reproduced in Carey and Whelton's synopsis, Ann Intern Med 2018;168:351. In this survey, the second occasion mattered more than the second reading.

Drug comparison. Law MR, Wald NJ, Morris JK, Jordan RE. Value of low dose combination treatment with blood pressure lowering drugs: analysis of 354 randomised trials. BMJ 2003;326:1427: the placebo-adjusted average fall at standard dose was mmHg systolic. The same paper found drugs lower BP more from higher starting levels (about 1 mmHg more per 10 mmHg), so the split above is a rough guide, not a fixed ratio.
