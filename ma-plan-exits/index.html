Medicare Advantage plans ending in 2027

By Usnish Majumdar, MD · Updated November 2026

Every fall, insurers decide which Medicare Advantage plans to keep, merge or drop for the next year, and CMS publishes a crosswalk of where each plan's members go. Some are moved into another plan automatically. Others find their plan is ending, or pulling out of their county, and must choose new coverage during open enrollment. This follows every member of an individual plan through that crosswalk, county by county.

    How this was estimated

    Members. Enrollment by plan and county comes from the CMS monthly enrollment file for September before each plan year, for individual Medicare Advantage plans (HMO, PPO, PFFS, including special needs plans). Employer group plans, PACE, cost plans and medical savings accounts are left out. CMS hides counts of 10 or fewer, so very small plans in a county are missed.

    Where they go. The CMS plan crosswalk (the version released October 1 each year) lists each plan's status for the coming year and the plan or plans its members move into. A member counts as moved to a plan only if that plan's next-year service area, from the CMS Plan Benefit Package files, includes their county. Members whose plan ends outright are counted as plan ended. Members whose plan continues elsewhere, but not in their county, are counted as plan leaving county. Members in both groups get a special enrollment period to choose new coverage. Without a choice, they end up in traditional Medicare.

    Plans and insurers. Counts of plans and insurers include only plans anyone eligible for Medicare in the county can join, not special needs plans. Regional PPOs count in every county of their Medicare Advantage region. Insurers are grouped by the parent company CMS lists for each contract, from the September enrollment file or, for contracts that are new next year, the CMS Landscape file. Some parents are not the brand members see: CMS lists Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan as the parent of WyoBlue Advantage in Wyoming, for example. Counties with 0–1 insurers are counted out of every county in the 50 states and DC; those with none include every Alaska borough, where no such plans are offered. Puerto Rico and the other territories are left out of every total and table.

    Caveats. The crosswalk is preliminary; a few plans change status after October. "Moved" members keep coverage without acting, but their new plan can have a different network, drug list and costs. Members who are moved can still switch during open enrollment. Plan service areas come from the October 1 benefit files; CMS added a few counties in November (Devoted Health in Carroll and Coos counties, New Hampshire, for 2027), which this does not include; both counties are counted with 0 insurers.

    Dual-eligible plans. Federal rules adopted in the 2025 Medicare Advantage final rule and taking effect in 2027 limit enrollment in some dual-eligible plans to people in the same insurer's Medicaid plan, and limit how many of these plans an insurer can offer where it runs a Medicaid plan (CMS, 42 CFR 422.514(h)).
