Does your nonprofit hospital earn its tax break?

Nonprofit hospitals pay no federal income tax in exchange for serving their communities. For every U.S. nonprofit acute care and critical access hospital, this compares the charity care it reported to Medicare in 2023 with the federal income tax it would have owed on its profits. The tax figure is a floor: state, property and sales tax exemptions are not counted.

    Dot size: estimated federal tax break (or hospital size when there is none). Counties shaded by share of residents without health insurance.

    About the data

    Hospitals and finances. CMS Hospital Provider Cost Report (HCRIS), 2023 release: nonprofit (church or other voluntary) short-term acute care and critical access hospitals in the 50 states and DC, using each hospital's latest 2023 report. Charity care is the cost (not charges) of free and discounted care reported on Worksheet S-10. Locations from the Census Bureau geocoder;

    Tax break estimate. 21% (the federal corporate rate) of each hospital's positive net income. This is deliberately conservative. It leaves out state income, property and sales tax exemptions, tax-exempt bond financing and tax-deductible donations, which together typically exceed the federal income tax piece. The Lown Institute's Fair Share Spending analysis estimates the full exemption from IRS filings and also counts community investment, not just charity care, so its results differ.

    Caveats. Cost-report net income includes investment gains, which swing year to year. Hospitals that are part of a larger system report separately here, even if the system files one tax return. Charity care is self-reported and definitions vary between hospitals. Counties' uninsured rates are CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index 2022 estimates (American Community Survey 2018–2022).
