Which door?

Same county, same paycheck, different hospital, different bill

Every nonprofit hospital sets its own income cutoff for free care. Nothing makes the hospitals in one county agree, and in many counties they don't. This page joins each nonprofit ER's cutoff, from its IRS filing, to the Census count of uninsured people and their incomes in the same county.

By Usnish Majumdar, MD · November 2026

    State free-care laws applied here

    Where a state requires every hospital to give free care below an income line, this page uses the higher of the hospital's reported cutoff and the state's line. Discount-only or price-cap laws are listed but not applied, because they do not make care free.

    About the data

    What is new here. Researchers have shown that nonprofit hospitals' free-care cutoffs vary widely and do not track community need: KFF (2022), Messac and colleagues, Health Affairs (2024), Santos and colleagues, Health Affairs (2026), Wong and colleagues, Health Services Research (2026), and the Lown Institute's charity care data and financial assistance policy report. The Lown Institute has also shown that neighboring hospitals can set very different lines, in Boston, Dallas, Pittsburgh and Waco ("Free care: Neighboring hospitals may have wildly different policies"). KFF's 2022 brief notes that how many low-income patients are eligible is not known. What this page adds is a nationwide, county-by-county comparison of nonprofit ERs and a count of the uninsured people whose income falls between their lines.
