    About the data

    Hospitals and finances. CMS Hospital Provider Cost Report (HCRIS). CMS's 2024 file holds cost reports for hospital fiscal years that began between ; the 2023 file, used only for the year-over-year comparison, holds years that began between . Included: nonprofit (church or other voluntary) short-term acute care and critical access hospitals in the 50 states and DC, using each hospital's latest report in the file. Government-owned and for-profit hospitals are not included. Charity care is the cost (not charges) of free and discounted care reported on Worksheet S-10.

    Medicaid expansion. Expansion status and implementation dates are from KFF's Status of State Action on the Medicaid Expansion Decision (). A state counts as having expanded only if expansion took effect before every one of its hospitals' report years began. Adult eligibility limits in non-expansion states are from KFF's Medicaid income eligibility limits for adults ().

    Counties. Uninsured and poverty rates (share of residents below 150% of the poverty line) are CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index 2022 estimates (American Community Survey 2018–2022), so they predate the Medicaid unwinding and the North Carolina and South Dakota expansions. Each hospital is placed in a county from its Census-geocoded address (ZIP-area center when the address doesn't match; hospitals with post-office-box addresses were placed by the county in CMS's hospital file).

    Tax exemption estimate. % (the federal corporate rate) of positive net income. It is a floor, not the full value of nonprofit status: it leaves out state and local property and sales tax exemptions (often the larger share), tax-exempt bond financing and deductible donations. Cost-report net income also includes investment gains that swing from year to year. The Lown Institute publishes a fuller analysis built on IRS filings.

    Charity care is one part of community benefit. Nonprofit hospitals also report other community benefit on IRS Form 990 Schedule H, including unreimbursed Medicaid costs, health professions education, research, subsidized services and community health programs. None of that is counted here. Charity care policies and reporting practices also differ between hospitals and states, and hospitals in a larger system report separately here.
