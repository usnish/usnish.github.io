Charity care at nonprofit hospitals

Nonprofit hospitals provide free and discounted care to patients who can't afford to pay, and in turn are exempt from most taxes. This map shows the charity care each U.S. nonprofit acute care and critical access hospital reported to Medicare for 2023, alongside how many people in its county lack health insurance.

    Each dot is a hospital, sized by charity care dollars and colored by charity care as a share of the hospital's total costs. Counties are shaded by the share of residents without health insurance.

    About the data

    Hospitals and finances. CMS Hospital Provider Cost Report (HCRIS), 2023 release: nonprofit (church or other voluntary) short-term acute care and critical access hospitals in the 50 states and DC, using each hospital's latest 2023 report. Charity care is the cost (not charges) of free and discounted care reported on Worksheet S-10. Locations from the Census Bureau geocoder;

    Charity care is one part of community benefit. Nonprofit hospitals also report other community benefit on IRS Form 990 Schedule H, including unreimbursed Medicaid costs, health professions education, research, subsidized services and community health programs. None of that is counted here, so charity care alone understates what many hospitals contribute.

    Tax exemption estimate. Each hospital's details include a rough estimate of its federal income tax exemption: 21% (the federal corporate rate) of positive net income. It leaves out state and local exemptions, tax-exempt bonds and deductible donations, and cost-report net income includes investment gains that swing from year to year, so it is context only. The Lown Institute publishes a fuller analysis built on IRS filings.

    Caveats. Charity care policies and reporting practices differ between hospitals and states, and states with broader Medicaid coverage tend to need less charity care. Hospitals in a larger system report separately here. County uninsured rates are CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index 2022 estimates (American Community Survey 2018–2022).
